Key Issues Identified

Usability Issues Found

Consolidated from heuristic evaluation and usability testing
High Severity H4 — Consistency

Inconsistent UI Components Across Modules

Button styles, colors, and interactive element behaviors vary significantly between the appointment booking, health records, and admin panel modules, creating confusion for users navigating between sections.

High Severity H10 — Help

Absence of In-App Help and Documentation

Users have no access to tooltips, tutorials, or contextual help within the system. First-time users — especially community members unfamiliar with digital health platforms — are left without guidance.

Medium Severity H3 — User Control

No Clear Navigation Back From Booking Flow

Once users enter the appointment booking sequence, there is no obvious way to cancel or go back without losing entered data, reducing users' sense of control over the system.

Medium Severity H1 — Visibility

Missing Feedback After User Actions

After submitting forms or completing actions, the system does not consistently provide confirmation messages or loading indicators, leaving users uncertain whether their action was successful.

Low Severity H7 — Flexibility

No Keyboard Shortcuts or Power-User Features

Experienced users or admin personnel have no efficiency shortcuts, forcing them to navigate the same steps as first-time users regardless of familiarity with the system.

Discussion

Discussion of Findings

The usability evaluation of the CommonWell Healthcare Management System — conducted with 40 respondents (24 male, 16 female; average age 20.6 years) — yielded an average SUS score of 55.75 out of 100, placing the system in the "Marginal" adjective category (Grade D). This indicates that while users were able to interact with the system, they encountered meaningful usability barriers that reduced their confidence and efficiency.

Item-level analysis reveals that Q7 (learnability, avg. 3.70) and Q1 (willingness to use frequently, avg. 3.65) scored highest, suggesting respondents viewed the system as relatively learnable and worth revisiting. However, Q6 (inconsistency, avg. 2.82) and Q2 (complexity, avg. 2.80) scored lowest — directly corroborating the high-severity heuristic violations found in H4 (Consistency) and H9 (Error Recovery). The majority of respondents (90%) used a smartphone to interact with the prototype, and 62.5% had no prior experience with similar systems, making ease of first-time use especially critical.

Open-ended feedback highlighted strengths in secure data sharing and appointment/progress tracking features. Respondents praised the system's ability to give healthcare providers quick access to patient histories — particularly valuable in emergency scenarios. However, multiple respondents suggested simplifying the interface, improving navigation, and enhancing data handling for unstructured content such as notes and images.

These findings are consistent with the heuristic evaluation results: while the aesthetic design (H8) and real-world language (H2) passed evaluation, the system's three high-severity failures in Consistency (H4), Error Recovery (H9), and Help & Documentation (H10) meaningfully degraded the overall user experience — particularly for the 25 respondents with no prior exposure to similar systems.
