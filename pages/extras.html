Reflections

Group Member Reflections

Personal insights and learning outcomes from the HCI evaluation process
Julie P. Marasigan · Group Leader

"Leading this evaluation taught me just how much the gap between a designer's intent and a user's experience can widen without proper usability testing. Watching respondents navigate the CommonWell prototype — some confidently, others hesitantly — made the SUS data feel real and personal. The process reinforced for me that good design is never finished; it's an ongoing conversation between builders and the people they build for. I leave this project with a deeper appreciation for iterative design and the value of listening before assuming."

Kim · Member

"Conducting the heuristic evaluation opened my eyes to issues I had completely overlooked as someone familiar with the system. Nielsen's 10 heuristics gave us a structured lens to evaluate what we otherwise might have dismissed. The H9 (Error Recovery) and H10 (Help & Documentation) failures surprised me the most — they seem like basics, yet they're so often neglected. Moving forward, I'll carry the habit of asking 'what happens when this breaks?' in every interface I design."

Jhonalyn · Member

"Administering the SUS questionnaire to 40 respondents was both humbling and enlightening. Many of them were first-time users of a healthcare management system, and their confusion with certain flows reminded me that we design for our users — not ourselves. The quantitative score of 55.75 felt like a challenge, not a verdict. It told us exactly where to improve. I'm grateful for a methodology that transforms subjective feelings into actionable numbers."

Clied Jude · Member

"Developing this website taught me that functionality is only half the battle; accessibility is what truly brings a platform to life. Our initial testing showed that while the features were robust, the navigation needed to be more intuitive for first-time users. Bridging that gap between complex code and a seamless user experience was the most challenging—yet fulfilling—part of this journey. It’s a reminder that we aren’t just building pages; we’re building pathways for information"

Anola · Member

"This project deepened my understanding of how people interact with health technology. The demographic data revealed that 62.5% of our respondents had no prior experience with similar platforms — a stark reminder that assumptions about digital literacy can undermine even well-intentioned designs. Documenting and analyzing the open-ended feedback was especially rewarding; the voice of the user, captured in their own words, is the most honest form of UX research."

Additional Findings

Supplementary Usability Findings

Observations and patterns not fully captured in the primary SUS results
1
Mobile Behavior

Smartphone Users Struggled Most With Form Inputs

90% of respondents accessed the prototype via smartphone, yet the appointment booking form fields were not optimized for mobile keyboards. Several respondents had to zoom in and scroll horizontally to complete forms — a friction point that likely contributed to the below-average H3 (User Control) and overall SUS score.

2
Task Completion

Appointment Booking Had the Highest Task Abandonment Rate

Informal observation during testing sessions noted that the appointment booking flow caused the most visible hesitation. Multiple participants paused on the date/time selection screen and expressed uncertainty about whether their selection had been registered — consistent with the H1 (Visibility of System Status) violation identified in heuristic evaluation.

3
Accessibility

Text Contrast Insufficient in Secondary Labels

During evaluation, two members independently flagged that muted gray text used in secondary labels and helper text did not meet WCAG 2.1 AA contrast ratio requirements (minimum 4.5:1 for body text). This is a silent barrier for respondents with visual impairments — a concern given the healthcare context of the platform.

4
Trust & Confidence

Users Expressed Uncertainty About Data Privacy

Several open-ended responses mentioned concerns about where their health data would be stored and who could access it. While the system includes a consent form, in-app trust signals (e.g., privacy indicators, data security badges, or clear messaging) are absent — a gap that may discourage adoption especially among community members new to digital health platforms.

5
Navigation

Admin Panel Was Perceived as Separate From the Main App

Several respondents who explored the admin-facing features commented that it "felt like a different website." The visual and navigational disconnect between the patient-facing and admin-facing portions of the prototype contributed to the H4 (Consistency & Standards) violation and reinforces the need for a unified design system across all user roles.

Prototype Walkthrough

CommonWell Feature Walkthrough

A guided tour of the key screens and flows tested during the evaluation

The CommonWell Healthcare Management System prototype was built in Figma and covers five core modules. Below is a step-by-step walkthrough of the flows evaluated by our 40 respondents.

🏠
Step 1 — Entry Point

Landing Screen & Login

Users arrive at the CommonWell landing page, which presents the platform's purpose and a login/sign-up prompt. The login flow asks for credentials and routes users to either the patient dashboard or the admin panel depending on their role.

📅
Step 2 — Core Flow

Appointment Booking

The patient selects "Book Appointment," chooses a healthcare service (e.g., general consultation, vaccination), picks an available date and time slot, and confirms the booking. This was the primary task given to most respondents and the highest-friction flow identified in the evaluation.

📋
Step 3 — Health Records

Viewing & Managing Health Records

Patients can access their health history, including past appointments, diagnoses, and prescriptions. The records view allows filtering by date and category. Evaluators noted that accessing specific records required more steps than expected, surfacing a minor navigation efficiency issue.

💊
Step 4 — Medication & Vaccines

Medication Tracker & Vaccination Schedule

This module displays the patient's active medications, dosage schedules, and upcoming vaccination dates. Respondents found the vaccination tracker visually clear but noted that medication management lacked reminders or alert mechanisms for missed doses.

🛠️
Step 5 — Admin Panel

Admin Panel & Patient Management

The admin view gives healthcare staff access to all patient records, appointment schedules, and system settings. Admin users can approve appointments, update health records, and manage user accounts. Evaluators flagged visual inconsistency between the admin UI and patient-facing modules as a high-severity issue.
